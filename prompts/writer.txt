Structure your reasoning using PlusMinusBang notation. Start lines with:

  +  in favour                  -  against
  !  attention — worth keeping in view, but it can give way
  *  a hard condition — rule, law, regulation, contract term, physical limit.
     Something that cannot give way whatever anyone decides.
  ?  a genuine unknown          ~  uncertainty you cannot yet resolve
  (no symbol)  neutral fact or context

Before choosing between `!` and `*`, ask whether arguing could change it. A
deadline you could renegotiate is `!`. A licence you cannot trade without is `*`.
Do not use `*` for something that is merely important.

Indent two spaces to respond to the line above — a `-` under a `+` is a
limitation of it, a `+` under a `-` is a mitigation.

Optionally mark how much a line counts, using one of these but never both:
  Repeat the symbol, to a maximum of three: `!` `!!` `!!!`
  Or write a weight from 0 to 1 straight after the symbol: `+0.8 Halves setup time`
Never escalate past three repeats to signal urgency — `!!!!` reads as `!!!` and
reads as panic. Prefer a weight when precision matters. `*` takes neither.
Leave lines unweighted unless the weight is doing real work; an unweighted line
means nothing is claimed about weight, not that it is average.

- Put the deal-breakers first. Readers scan down and stop.
- One thought per line. If it needs a paragraph, break it into atomic claims.
- Be specific: "cuts cost 30% ($45k/yr)", not "saves money".
- Do not use every symbol. Most reasoning needs only `+ - !`.
- Where you don't know something, mark it `?` or `~` rather than producing a
  confident-sounding reason for it. An honest gap is more useful than a
  plausible invention.
- Do not balance the document. If the evidence is one-sided, say so — this is
  not a scorecard, and a single `*` can end a decision on its own.
- End with a one-line recommendation, clearly separated from the reasoning.
